Textual check of the last centroid step, for use in the existing universal-theorem submission. Let K be any compact convex body in R^n, V its positive volume, and c=V^{-1} integral_K x dx. First c belongs to K. Otherwise choose a nearest point y in K to c. For each x in K, the segment y+t(x-y) is in K for 0<=t<=1. Minimality at t=0 of |c-y-t(x-y)|^2 gives (c-y) dot (x-y)<=0. Integrating over K yields V|c-y|^2<=0, a contradiction.

Suppose next that c is a boundary point of K. Choose z_j outside K with z_j->c and choose a nearest y_j in K to z_j. Since c is in K, |z_j-y_j|<=|z_j-c|, so y_j->c. Set v_j=(z_j-y_j)/|z_j-y_j| and pass to a subsequence with v_j->v, |v|=1. The same segment derivative gives v_j dot (x-y_j)<=0 for every x in K. Taking limits gives v dot (x-c)<=0 on K. Choose a closed ball B(a,r) contained in K with r>0. Since a+rv belongs to K, v dot(a-c)<=-r; hence v dot(x-c)<=-r/2 throughout B(a,r/2), a positive-volume subset. Thus integral_K v dot(x-c) dx<0, contradicting the defining identity integral_K(x-c)dx=0. Therefore c is interior.

F006 now applies to K-c. Its C1 function B has a maximum u on S^(n-1); every tangent directional derivative there is zero, so its spherical gradient is zero. F006 gives integral_((K-c) intersect u-perp) x dH^(n-1)=0 and a positive finite denominator. Consequently c(K intersect(c+u-perp))=c. This deduction is independent of any boundary regularity, of B being nonconstant, and of the number of distinct extrema. It proves at least one geometric section once admitted as part of the universal candidate, without interpreting antipodal normals as two sections.
