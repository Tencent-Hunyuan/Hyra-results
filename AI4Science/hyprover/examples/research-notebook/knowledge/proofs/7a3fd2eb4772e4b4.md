Fix an arbitrary integer n>=3. Every sphere, round metric and surface measure in the proof is the Euclidean one specified in the statement. We use exactly the following four published project predecessors, and no external results:
83fc8a6190cb2ca3 (smooth odd twisted heights),
a0c54399c2d44b8d (finite harmonic approximation and C2 critical-point stability),
e9b3873b7c826c7b (finite odd harmonic inversion of H), and
dc5ba3bf489a3dea (centered radial perturbation realization).
Each is valid for this same arbitrary n.

By fact 83fc8a6190cb2ca3 there is a real smooth odd function F_n on S^{n-1} satisfying
integral_{S^{n-1}} theta F_n(theta) d sigma(theta)=0,
with exactly two critical points, e_1 and -e_1, and with spherical Hessians equal respectively to the negative and positive round metric. Hence F_n is C2 and its two critical points are nondegenerate. These are every input hypothesis in parts (1) and (2) of fact a0c54399c2d44b8d.

Apply the final conclusion of part (2) of a0c54399c2d44b8d to F_n, for example with requested C2 approximation tolerance 1. It supplies a real finite sum P of spherical harmonics of odd degrees at least 3 such that P has exactly two nondegenerate critical points, forming one antipodal pair. In particular P is smooth and odd, and has no degree-one harmonic component. Its actual approximation error need not be specified: the cited conclusion already supplies simultaneous approximation and preservation of the complete critical set. There is no assertion that every approximation within tolerance 1 preserves that set.

Group the finitely many components of P by degree, writing
P=sum_{j=1}^J P_{2j+1},
where each P_{2j+1} is a real spherical harmonic of degree 2j+1 and missing degrees may be represented by zero. Such J is finite. Since a function with exactly two critical points is not the zero function, P is not the empty sum.

The hypotheses of the finite inversion clause in e9b3873b7c826c7b are now satisfied: P is a real odd finite harmonic sum with no degree-one component, and the transform H in that fact uses exactly the same Euclidean measure and sign convention as here. Define
g=sum_{j=1}^J lambda_{n,2j+1}^{-1} P_{2j+1},
where lambda_{n,2j+1} are the nonzero real eigenvalues supplied by e9b3873b7c826c7b. That fact gives all of the following conclusions: g is a real smooth odd finite harmonic sum, it has only odd degrees at least 3, Hg=P exactly, and
integral_{S^{n-1}} theta g(theta) d sigma(theta)=0.
In particular Hg has precisely the two nondegenerate critical points already established for P. No passage from inversion on finite sums to inversion on an arbitrary smooth function is used.

Consequently this g meets every input hypothesis of dc5ba3bf489a3dea: n>=3; real C-infinity regularity; oddness; exact zero vector first moment; and exactly two nondegenerate critical points of Hg. That fact supplies epsilon_0>0 such that for every real epsilon with 0<|epsilon|<epsilon_0 the specified radial function is positive and the resulting K_epsilon is a compact convex body with nonempty interior, C-infinity boundary, positive principal curvatures and the stated strict convexity. It gives c(K_epsilon)=0 exactly, and exactly one geometric affine hyperplane with positive section measure and section centroid 0.

For clarity, the uniqueness conclusion being applied counts all affine hyperplanes, not only a preferred family. If A={x:u dot x=b} with |u|=1 has section centroid 0, then integration of u dot x=b over its positive-measure section gives b=0. Thus every candidate is central. The same predecessor identifies the central-section first moment with one half the spherical gradient of the signed halfspace-volume function, and proves that this function has exactly two nondegenerate antipodal critical points. Antipodal normals define the same geometric hyperplane. Positive radial function gives positive measure for every central section. These facts establish the exact geometric count asserted here, with no translation or subsequent smoothing of K_epsilon.

Finally choose any epsilon in (0,epsilon_0) to obtain the first assertion with K=K_epsilon. The more precise family assertion and the stated properties of g were proved at the preceding steps. Since n was arbitrary throughout and every predecessor covers all n>=3, the conclusion holds for every required dimension.
