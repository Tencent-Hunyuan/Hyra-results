We construct the lift and the homotopy explicitly. All integrals below use ordinary Euclidean surface measure.

1. A consequence of the input homotopy. Each U_s has global maximum 1 and global minimum -1. Indeed a smooth function on the compact sphere has a maximum and minimum, both are critical, and by hypothesis its two critical values are exactly 1 and -1. Oddness interchanges these points. At any critical point omega of U_s, one has partial_s U_s(omega)=0. To justify this last identity, nondegeneracy and the implicit function theorem continue each critical point to a smooth local curve omega(s) of critical points. The critical value along that curve is locally the same member of {1,-1}, hence is constant. Differentiating U_s(omega(s)) and using d_omega U_s=0 proves the identity. The argument holds by one-sided continuation at the endpoints and by continuity of partial_s U_s there. In particular |U_s|<=1 for every s.

2. Smooth suspension before the moment correction. Write r=|x|=sqrt(1-t^2) and omega=x/r away from the two points r=0. Fix a C-infinity function b:[0,infinity)->[0,1] with b=1 on [0,1] and b=0 on [2,infinity). For 0<delta<1/4 put beta_delta(r)=b(r/delta). Define
A_delta(x,t)=r U_{beta_delta(r)}(omega) when r>0,
and set A_delta(0,1)=A_delta(0,-1)=0. When r<=delta the formula reduces exactly to r omega_1=x_1. It follows that A_delta is smooth at both poles as well as elsewhere. It is odd in (x,t) and even in t, because every U_s is odd and r depends only on t^2. When r>=2delta it equals rF(omega), in particular on an entire neighborhood of the equator.

We check all critical points. On -1<t<1, (omega,t) are smooth product coordinates and r>0. The angular differential of A_delta is r d_omega U_{beta_delta(r)}. Thus a critical point must be an angular critical point of that U_s. At such a point U_s=epsilon, where epsilon is 1 or -1, and partial_s U_s=0 by step 1. The t derivative of A_delta at that point is therefore r'(t)epsilon=-epsilon t/r; the term containing the derivative of beta_delta vanishes. This is zero only for t=0. Near t=0 beta_delta=0, so the two critical points are precisely (p,0) and (-p,0). At either pole A_delta=x_1, whose spherical differential is nonzero there (the tangent vector in the x_1 direction has derivative 1). Thus no pole is critical.

Near (q,0), with q=p or -p, A_delta=sqrt(1-t^2)F(omega). The angular Hessian at the critical point is Hess F(q); the mixed terms vanish since r'(0)=0; and the t-t entry is r''(0)F(q)=-F(q). At a critical point the coordinate second derivative is the intrinsic Hessian, and the angular and t tangent directions at t=0 are orthogonal unit factors. This proves the Hessian direct-sum formula. Both blocks are nonsingular, so the two critical points are nondegenerate.

3. The moment error tends to zero. Define a continuous reference function A_ref by A_ref(x,t)=rF(omega) for r>0 and A_ref=0 at the poles. In product coordinates the surface element of S^d is r^{d-2}d sigma_{d-1}(omega)dt: the angular tangent directions contribute r^{d-1}, while the t direction has length 1/r and is orthogonal to them. Hence
integral_{S^d} x A_ref d sigma_d = (integral_{-1}^{1}r^d dt)(integral_{S^{d-1}}omega F(omega)d sigma_{d-1})=0.
Its t moment is zero by t-reflection, since A_ref(x,-t)=A_ref(x,t). Thus its full first moment vanishes. The difference A_delta-A_ref is supported where r<2delta and is bounded in absolute value by 2r<=4delta. Consequently A_delta converges uniformly to A_ref as delta decreases to zero. It follows directly from |x|<=1 and finiteness of surface area that
m_delta:=integral_{S^d}x A_delta(x,t)d sigma_d tends to zero in R^d.
The last-coordinate moment of A_delta is exactly zero by its evenness in t.

4. Correct the moment in a region with a fixed gradient bound. Choose a nonnegative, nonzero, even C-infinity function chi on [-1,1] supported in {t:1/3<|t|<2/3}. Set
c_chi=integral_{S^d}chi(t)x_1^2 d sigma_d>0.
Reflections in the x coordinates show that integral chi(t)x_i x_j d sigma_d=0 for i!=j, and orthogonal coordinate permutations show that the diagonal integrals all equal c_chi. Also integral t chi(t)x_j d sigma_d=0, by reflection in x_j. Thus for any a in R^d the first moment of the smooth function Q_a(x,t)=chi(t)(a dot x) is exactly (c_chi a,0).

Let K_band={ (x,t) in S^d:1/3<=|t|<=2/3 }. This is compact and avoids the poles and equator. On this set A_ref is smooth with no critical point: its angular differential can vanish only where F=1 or -1, and there its t derivative is -tF/r, which is nonzero. Therefore there is kappa>0 such that |grad A_ref|>=kappa on K_band. For every delta<1/4, r>=sqrt(5)/3>2delta on this band, so A_delta=A_ref in a neighborhood of it. The spherical gradient of Q_a is bounded everywhere by (||chi'||_infinity+||chi||_infinity)|a|: apply the product rule and the bounds |x|<=1, |grad t|<=1, |grad(a dot x)|<=|a|.

Set a_delta=m_delta/c_chi. Since a_delta tends to zero, choose delta sufficiently small that the displayed gradient bound for Q_{a_delta} is less than kappa/2. Define
G=A_delta-Q_{a_delta}.
Its first moment is zero by the exact moment calculation. It is odd in (x,t) and even in t. On K_band, the spherical gradient of A_delta-lambda Q_{a_delta} is nonzero for every lambda in [0,1], by the triangle inequality and the kappa bound. Off K_band this function equals A_delta because chi is supported inside the band. Therefore every function A_delta-lambda Q_{a_delta} has exactly the two critical points (p,0),(-p,0); their values and Hessians are unchanged because the correction vanishes on a neighborhood of the equator. In particular G has all the asserted critical-point properties and restricts to F at t=0.

5. Preserve the homotopy property. The path lambda -> A_delta-(1-lambda)Q_{a_delta} joins G to A_delta through smooth odd functions with the same two nondegenerate critical points and critical values 1,-1. Next, for v in [0,1], set
B_v(x,t)=r U_{v+(1-v)beta_delta(r)}(omega) for r>0,
with value 0 at the poles. This is jointly smooth in v and (x,t), since near the poles its parameter is 1 and it equals x_1. It joins A_delta at v=0 to x_1 at v=1. At any angular critical point of U_s the derivative partial_s U_s is zero by step 1. The identical differentiation from step 2 shows that the only critical points of B_v lie at t=0 and correspond exactly to the two critical points of U_v. In a neighborhood of the equator beta_delta=0, so the Hessian blocks there are Hess U_v and minus its critical value. They are nonsingular, and the critical values remain 1,-1. The poles are again regular because B_v=x_1 there. These functions are odd throughout.

Concatenate these two paths, using smooth nondecreasing reparametrizations that are constant near each endpoint, to obtain a jointly C-infinity homotopy from G to x_1 with the required properties. This reparametrization preserves every pointwise-in-parameter property just proved and makes the concatenation smooth. We have proved that the full input homotopy condition is inherited by the output. Applying this construction finitely many times proves the statement for every D>=d; the restriction to the original coordinate subsphere is preserved at each step. The case D=d uses F itself. No project fact or external result beyond the elementary differential and integral arguments given here is used.
