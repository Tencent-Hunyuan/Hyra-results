We use the published fact 8f8adadaf10ea26e, "Signed halfspace volume is C1 and its spherical gradient is twice the central-section first moment." It applies for every n>=2 to K-p whenever p is an interior point, without boundary regularity.

First we prove centroid interiority independently of that application. Let V=vol_n(K), which is finite and positive by compactness and nonempty interior. Choose a in int(K) and r>0 such that the closed Euclidean ball B(a,r) is contained in K. Let Q=B(a,r/2) and set delta=r vol_n(Q)/(2V)>0. For each unit vector u let h_K(u)=max_{x in K} u dot x, which exists by compactness. The point a+ru belongs to K, so h_K(u)>=u dot a+r. Every x in Q satisfies u dot x<=u dot a+r/2. The integrand h_K(u)-u dot x is nonnegative on all of K and is at least r/2 on Q. Consequently
h_K(u)-u dot c(K)
=(1/V)integral_K (h_K(u)-u dot x) d vol_n(x)
>=delta
for every unit u.

For completeness, K is exactly the intersection of the closed halfspaces {z:u dot z<=h_K(u)} over unit u. One inclusion follows from the definition of h_K. For the other, take z not in K and choose q in K minimizing |z-q|; such q exists since K is compact and |z-q|>0. For each x in K and each t in [0,1], convexity gives q+t(x-q) in K. The function |z-q-t(x-q)|^2 is minimized over this interval at t=0, so its right derivative there is nonnegative:
-2(z-q) dot (x-q)>=0.
With u=(z-q)/|z-q|, this gives u dot x<=u dot q for every x in K. Thus h_K(u)=u dot q<u dot z, excluding z from that halfspace. This proves the asserted representation. If |z-c(K)|<delta, then for every unit u,
u dot z<=u dot c(K)+|z-c(K)|<h_K(u).
The representation implies z in K. Hence the open ball of radius delta about c(K) lies in K, proving c(K) in int(K).

Now fix an arbitrary p in int(K), and put L=K-p. Fact 8f8adadaf10ea26e shows that L is a positive continuous radial body and that the signed halfspace-volume function B_L is odd and C^1, with
grad_S B_L(u)=2 integral_{L intersect u^\perp} y d H^{n-1}(y).
Translation invariance of Euclidean volume gives B_L=B_{K,p}. Because L contains a ball of some radius r_p>0 about zero, every central section L intersect u^\perp contains an (n-1)-dimensional ball of radius r_p. It therefore has positive (n-1)-measure. The section is bounded, so its measure is finite. These statements also follow from the positive section-area formula in 8f8adadaf10ea26e.

Compactness of S^{n-1} and continuity of B_L give a maximum point u_0. For every tangent vector v in u_0^\perp the curve
t -> (u_0+t v)/sqrt(1+t^2|v|^2)
lies on the sphere and passes through u_0 with velocity v. The differentiable function obtained by restricting B_L to this curve has a maximum at t=0, so its derivative is zero. Since this holds for all v, grad_S B_L(u_0)=0. The gradient formula implies
integral_{L intersect u_0^\perp} y d H^{n-1}(y)=0.
Its section area is positive, so c(L intersect u_0^\perp)=0. Translation sends this section to K intersect (p+u_0^\perp), preserves its measure, and adds p to its centroid. Thus c(K intersect (p+u_0^\perp))=p. This proves existence for every prescribed interior p.

We check the exact correspondence and orientation issue. If an affine hyperplane is written A={x:u dot x=b} with |u|=1, and its section S=K intersect A has positive measure, then
u dot c(S)
=(1/H^{n-1}(S)) integral_S (u dot x) d H^{n-1}(x)
=b.
Therefore c(S) lies in A. If c(S)=p, then b=u dot p and A=p+u^\perp. Conversely every plane p+u^\perp has positive section measure as already proved, and translation plus the gradient identity give
c(K intersect (p+u^\perp))=p
if and only if integral_{L intersect u^\perp} y d H^{n-1}(y)=0
if and only if grad_S B_{K,p}(u)=0.
The gradient vector is even under u -> -u by 8f8adadaf10ea26e, so its zero set is antipodally invariant. Two unit normals specify the same linear hyperplane exactly when they differ by sign: the orthogonal complement of that hyperplane is a one-dimensional vector space. Thus the stated map on antipodal pairs is both injective and surjective onto the matching geometric affine hyperplanes. In particular the maximum and its antipodal minimum are not counted as two different hyperplanes.

Finally c(K) is an admissible interior p by the first part. Applying existence with this choice yields at least one centroid-matching section for every K in every n>=2. This is precisely the claimed universal lower bound, with unoriented counting.
