We use exactly the active project predecessors
3ce5f83832ddd38c (sharp planar centroid-section theorem),
a8378dfc6c55d238 (every interior point is the centroid of a hyperplane section), and
7a3fd2eb4772e4b4 (smooth strictly convex examples with exactly one centroid-matching hyperplane for every n>=3).
All three statements use ordinary Euclidean volume and section measures and count geometric affine hyperplanes without orientation, as in this statement. No external assertion is used.

First let n>=2 and let K be any convex body in R^n. Fact a8378dfc6c55d238 states that c(K) belongs to int(K) and that for every p in int(K) there is a positive-measure hyperplane section with centroid p. Apply it with p=c(K). This gives at least one such section for every K and proves the universal lower bound mu(n)>=1. The predecessor imposes no boundary regularity, so this step applies also to all nonsmooth convex bodies.

Now fix any integer n>=3. Fact 7a3fd2eb4772e4b4 supplies a compact convex body K in R^n with nonempty interior, C-infinity boundary, positive principal curvatures, the exact strict-convexity property in the statement, and centroid 0. It has exactly one geometric affine hyperplane A with positive section measure and section centroid 0. Since its body centroid is also 0, this is exactly one centroid-matching section. This proves the required smooth strictly convex existence assertion for this arbitrary n. It also shows that no integer q>=2 can have the defining universal property of mu(n), since this particular K has only one such hyperplane. Therefore mu(n)<=1. Combining with the universal bound already proved gives mu(n)=1 for every n>=3.

Finally let n=2. Fact 3ce5f83832ddd38c applies to every compact planar convex body with nonempty interior and supplies at least three distinct geometric affine lines with positive-length sections whose length centroids equal the body's area centroid. Thus q=3 has the defining universal property and mu(2)>=3. The same fact proves that every nondegenerate triangle has exactly three such lines. A nondegenerate triangle is a convex body, so no integer q>=4 has the defining universal property. Therefore mu(2)<=3 and mu(2)=3.

The lower and upper bounds show in particular that the stated largest integers exist. The n>=3 examples and the universal bounds in every dimension, together with both planar bounds, establish every asserted clause.
