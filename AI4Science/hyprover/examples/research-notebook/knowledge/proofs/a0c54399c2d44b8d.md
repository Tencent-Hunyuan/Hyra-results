We give an explicit polynomial approximation, its harmonic decomposition, the required local and global stability bounds, and the C^1 counterexample. No approximation theorem for spherical harmonics is assumed.

1. Polynomial kernels approximate in C^2.

For each integer m >= 1 define
K_m(x,y) = ((1+x dot y)/2)^m,  x,y in S,
Z_m = integral_S K_m(x,y) d sigma(y),
and
T_m h(x) = Z_m^{-1} integral_S K_m(x,y) h(y) d sigma(y).
Orthogonal changes of variables show that Z_m is independent of x; it is positive. The same integral formula, with x allowed to range over R^n and with the same constant Z_m, defines a polynomial in x of degree at most m with real coefficients whenever h is real and continuous.

We first prove uniform approximation for every continuous h on S. Fix 0 < d < 1. Since |x-y|^2 = 2-2x dot y, K_m(x,y) = (1-|x-y|^2/4)^m on S. On the set |x-y| >= d its base is at most a_d = 1-d^2/4. On the cap |x-y| <= d/2 its base is at least b_d = 1-d^2/16. The cap has a positive area c_d independent of x, and 0 < a_d < b_d. It follows that
Z_m >= c_d b_d^m
and
Z_m^{-1} integral_{|x-y| >= d} K_m(x,y) d sigma(y)
 <= (A/c_d) (a_d/b_d)^m.
Let omega_h(d) = sup{|h(x)-h(y)| : x,y in S, |x-y| <= d}. Positivity and normalization of the kernel give
||T_m h-h||_infinity
 <= omega_h(d) + 2 ||h||_infinity (A/c_d)(a_d/b_d)^m.
Uniform continuity first lets d be chosen small, and then the ratio a_d/b_d < 1 lets m be chosen large. Thus T_m h -> h uniformly.

For 1 <= i < j <= n let X_{ij} be the rotation vector field
X_{ij}(x) = x_i e_j - x_j e_i,
and also write X_{ij} for directional differentiation along it. Its flow is a Euclidean rotation. For every rotation R, invariance of surface measure gives
T_m(h composed with R) = (T_m h) composed with R.
Differentiating this identity along a rotation flow is justified under the integral for h in C^1, since the sphere is compact and the differentiated integrand is continuous. Consequently
X_{ij} T_m h = T_m X_{ij} h.
For F in C^2, applying this identity twice also gives
X_{ij} X_{kl} T_m F = T_m(X_{ij} X_{kl} F).
The functions F, X_{ij}F, and X_{ij}X_{kl}F are continuous. The uniform approximation already proved therefore controls T_m F-F and all these first and second rotation derivatives.

Here is an explicit justification that these controls imply convergence in the intrinsic C^2 norm. Index the N=n(n-1)/2 rotation fields by a single index a. For x in S and v in T_x S, direct expansion gives
v = sum_a <v,X_a(x)> X_a(x),
sum_a <v,X_a(x)>^2 = |v|^2.
In particular, |grad_S u(x)|^2 = sum_a (X_a u(x))^2. Write X_b(x)=A_b x, where A_b is its constant skew-symmetric matrix of operator norm 1. The spherical covariant derivative is the tangential projection of the ambient derivative, so
nabla_{X_a} X_b = projection_{T_x S}(A_b A_a x),
whose norm is at most 1. Therefore
Hess_S u(X_a,X_b) = X_a X_b u - du(nabla_{X_a} X_b).
If D_1 = max_a ||X_a u||_infinity and D_2 = max_{a,b} ||X_a X_b u||_infinity, this identity and the preceding expansions yield
||grad_S u||_infinity <= sqrt(N) D_1,
||Hess_S u||_{op,infinity} <= N (D_2 + sqrt(N) D_1).
Indeed, expand two unit tangent arguments in the X_a and use that each resulting coefficient vector has Euclidean norm 1, hence sum of absolute values at most sqrt(N). Applying these bounds to u=T_m F-F proves ||T_m F-F||_{C^2} -> 0.

If F is odd, the ambient polynomial q_m given by the integral formula satisfies q_m(-x)=-q_m(x): replace y by -y and use F(-y)=-F(y). Hence q_m has only odd homogeneous components. Its restriction Q_m=T_m F is an odd polynomial approximation to F in C^2.

2. Finite homogeneous-harmonic decomposition.

We prove the polynomial decomposition used here. Write r^2=x_1^2+...+x_n^2. Let q be homogeneous of degree k, and put l=floor(k/2). For j >= 1 and a homogeneous polynomial p of degree k-2j, the product rule and Euler's identity x dot grad p=(k-2j)p give
Delta(r^{2j}p)
 = r^{2j} Delta p + 2j(2k+n-2j-2) r^{2j-2}p.
Set c_0=1 and, for 1 <= j <= l, define
c_j = -c_{j-1}/[2j(2k+n-2j-2)].
The denominators are positive for n>=3 and this range of j. The homogeneous polynomial
h_k = sum_{j=0}^l c_j r^{2j} Delta^j q
is harmonic. In its Laplacian the coefficient of each r^{2j-2} Delta^j q is c_{j-1}+2j(2k+n-2j-2)c_j=0, and the last unpaired term vanishes because Delta^{l+1}q=0. For k=0 or 1 the assertion holds directly with h_k=q.

Since q-h_k is divisible by r^2, write q=h_k+r^2 q_{k-2} with q_{k-2} homogeneous of degree k-2. Induction on k gives
q = h_k + r^2 h_{k-2} + r^4 h_{k-4} + ...,
with each indicated h harmonic and homogeneous of its indicated degree. On S, r=1, so q restricts to a finite sum of spherical harmonics of these degrees. Applying this separately to the odd homogeneous components of q_m shows that Q_m is a finite sum of odd spherical harmonics, possibly including degree 1.

3. Removing precisely degree 1 while retaining convergence.

Reflection symmetry gives integral_S x_i x_j d sigma = 0 for i != j. Permutation symmetry and sum_i x_i^2=1 give integral_S x_i^2 d sigma=A/n. Thus Pi_1 is the identity on linear restrictions and is a projection onto them.

If H_k is homogeneous harmonic of degree k != 1, apply the divergence theorem in the unit ball to H_k grad x_i - x_i grad H_k. Its divergence is zero. On S the outward normal derivative of x_i is x_i, while that of H_k is k H_k by homogeneity. Hence
0 = (1-k) integral_S x_i H_k(x) d sigma(x).
Thus every harmonic degree k != 1 has zero first moment, and Pi_1 annihilates it. It follows that subtracting Pi_1 Q_m removes exactly the aggregate degree-1 component in the finite decomposition and leaves only odd degrees at least 3.

For a vector a in R^n, the linear restriction L_a(x)=a dot x has
grad_S L_a = a-(a dot x)x,
Hess_S L_a = -(a dot x) times the round metric.
Both formulas follow by differentiating the displayed gradient and projecting onto the tangent space. Therefore ||L_a||_{C^2} <= 3|a|. For a=(n/A) integral_S y u(y) d sigma(y), the triangle inequality gives |a| <= n||u||_infinity. This proves ||Pi_1 u||_{C^2} <= 3n||u||_infinity.

The zero first moment of F says Pi_1 F=0. Set
P_m=Q_m-Pi_1 Q_m.
Then
||P_m-F||_{C^2}
 <= ||Q_m-F||_{C^2}+||Pi_1(Q_m-F)||_{C^2}
 <= ||Q_m-F||_{C^2}+3n||Q_m-F||_infinity -> 0.
The same argument proves the stated bound for any odd polynomial restriction Q. All P_m are smooth, real, odd, and finite sums of odd harmonics of degrees at least 3. Their first moments vanish by the divergence calculation. This proves (1).

4. Quantitative local and global stability when there are two critical points.

Let f have exactly two critical points and let both be nondegenerate. It is not constant. A maximum and a minimum exist by compactness, are distinct, and are critical. Thus these are precisely the two critical points; denote them p_+ and p_-. The spherical Hessian is negative semidefinite at p_+ and positive semidefinite at p_-. Nondegeneracy makes these forms negative definite and positive definite, respectively.

Choose smooth coordinate maps phi_+ and phi_- from neighborhoods of 0 in R^{n-1} to S, with phi_+(0)=p_+, phi_-(0)=p_-, and with disjoint images of sufficiently small closed coordinate balls. For example, after choosing an isometry U from R^{n-1} onto p^\perp, the map phi(z)=sqrt(1-|z|^2)p+Uz is such a chart near p. At the critical center, the coordinate Hessian is the spherical Hessian pulled back by the invertible differential of the chart. By continuity, there are common numbers r>0 and lambda>0 such that the charts are defined beyond the closed ball of radius r, their closed-ball images are disjoint, and
D^2(f composed with phi_-)(z) >= 2 lambda I,
D^2(f composed with phi_+)(z) <= -2 lambda I
for all |z|<=r, in the sense of quadratic forms.

There is a constant C>=1, depending only on these two charts and closed balls, such that for every C^2 u on S,
sup_{|z|<=r} ||D^2(u composed with phi_+)(z)||_{op} <= C||u||_{C^2},
and the same holds for phi_-.
To justify this bound, for coordinate vectors a,b use
D^2(u composed with phi)(a,b)
 = Hess_S u(Dphi a,Dphi b)
   + du(projection_{T_phi(z) S} D^2 phi(a,b)).
The first and second derivatives of each fixed smooth chart are bounded on the compact ball. This gives the claimed C and uses both the first and second intrinsic derivatives of u.

Let U_+ and U_- be the images of the open coordinate balls of radius r. On the compact complement C_0=S minus (U_+ union U_-), f has no critical point. Hence
gamma = min_{C_0} |grad_S f| > 0.
Choose
delta_f = min{lambda/C, lambda r^2/4, gamma/2}.
All three entries are positive. Suppose ||g-f||_{C^2}<delta_f. The coordinate Hessian bound gives
D^2(g composed with phi_-) >= lambda I,
D^2(g composed with phi_+) <= -lambda I
throughout the closed balls. Thus these coordinate functions are strictly convex and strictly concave, respectively.

The Hessian bounds for f and D(f composed with phi_+)(0)=D(f composed with phi_-)(0)=0 imply, by twice integrating along the segment from 0 to z,
(f composed with phi_+)(z) <= f(p_+) - lambda r^2,
(f composed with phi_-)(z) >= f(p_-) + lambda r^2
when |z|=r. Since ||g-f||_infinity<delta_f, these inequalities give
(g composed with phi_+)(0) > (g composed with phi_+)(z),
(g composed with phi_-)(0) < (g composed with phi_-)(z)
on the respective boundaries: the gap is at least lambda r^2-2delta_f >= lambda r^2/2. Compactness of each closed ball therefore supplies an interior maximum or minimum for the corresponding coordinate function.

There is at most one critical point in each ball. For instance, if z,w were distinct critical points of a function v with D^2v>=lambda I, then
(grad v(z)-grad v(w)) dot (z-w)
 = integral_0^1 (z-w)^T D^2v(w+t(z-w))(z-w) dt
 >= lambda |z-w|^2 > 0,
contrary to the vanishing of both gradients. The concave case follows by applying this argument to -v. The definite coordinate Hessians show the two interior critical points are nondegenerate; at a critical point the coordinate Hessian and intrinsic Hessian are related by an invertible change of tangent coordinates.

Finally, on C_0,
|grad_S g| >= |grad_S f|-|grad_S(g-f)| > gamma-delta_f >= gamma/2,
so there are no other critical points. This proves the stability assertion. If g is odd, let a(x)=-x. Differentiating g composed with a=-g shows that a carries every critical point to another critical point. It has no fixed point on S. Exactly two critical points must therefore be antipodal.

Apply this stability assertion to F in (1). Since P_m -> F in C^2, any sufficiently large m has
||P_m-F||_{C^2}<min{epsilon,delta_F}.
The function P=P_m satisfies every assertion in (2).

5. Why C^1 is insufficient even for odd perturbations.

Choose a fixed smooth odd function eta on R with eta(t)=1 for 3/4<=t<=1 and eta(t)=-1 for -1<=t<=-3/4. Such a function can be constructed explicitly: let b(s)=exp(-1/s) for s>0 and b(s)=0 for s<=0, put
chi(t)=b(t-1/2)/(b(t-1/2)+b(3/4-t)),
and set eta(t)=chi(t)-chi(-t).
The denominator is positive everywhere, and this construction has the stated smoothness and constant values.

For epsilon>0 put
g_epsilon(x)=x_n-epsilon^3 eta(x_n) cos(x_1/epsilon^2).
These functions are smooth and odd. The perturbation has absolute value at most epsilon^3||eta||_infinity, and its spherical gradient has norm at most
epsilon^3||eta'||_infinity + epsilon||eta||_infinity,
where the norms can be taken on [-1,1]; this follows by first differentiating the ambient expression and then projecting tangentially. Thus g_epsilon -> (x -> x_n) in C^1.

In the north-pole chart x=(z_1,...,z_{n-1},sqrt(1-|z|^2)), for |z|<1/2 we have eta(x_n)=1, and the coordinate function is
sqrt(1-|z|^2)-epsilon^3 cos(z_1/epsilon^2).
At z=(t,0,...,0), all its derivatives in directions z_j with j>=2 vanish, and its derivative in direction z_1 equals
D_epsilon(t) = -t/sqrt(1-t^2)+epsilon sin(t/epsilon^2).
This is odd and vanishes at t=0. At a_epsilon=pi epsilon^2/2 it is positive for all sufficiently small epsilon, since a_epsilon/sqrt(1-a_epsilon^2)=O(epsilon^2). At b_epsilon=3 pi epsilon^2/2 it is negative. For sufficiently small epsilon, 0<a_epsilon<b_epsilon<1/2. The intermediate value theorem supplies a root s_epsilon in (a_epsilon,b_epsilon), and oddness supplies the root -s_epsilon. Hence the north-pole chart contains at least three distinct critical points. The oddness of g_epsilon supplies their three distinct antipodes in the south-pole chart, for at least six critical points altogether.

The height x_n has gradient e_n-x_n x, so its only critical points are +/-e_n; its Hessians there are respectively minus and plus the round metric. Thus they are nondegenerate, proving (3). The stability proof in part 4 uses a gradient bound on the complement of critical neighborhoods and a genuine Hessian bound inside them; this counterexample explains why the latter cannot simply be omitted.
