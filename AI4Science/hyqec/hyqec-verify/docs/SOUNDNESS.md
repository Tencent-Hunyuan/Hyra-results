# Soundness: what each hyqec-verify result proves, and why

This document states exactly what is proved by every kind of evidence hyqec-verify
produces, which code has to be trusted for it, and what is *not* proved.

*Names.* hyqec-verify was developed as *stabcert*. **File formats keep their historical `stabcert-*`
ids** (`stabcert-certificate/1`, `stabcert-pauli-cnf/1`, `stabcert-group/1`, `stabcert-bnb-instance 1`,
`stabcert-bnb-coverage/1` / `SBCOV1`, `stabcert-bnb-ckpt 2`): these strings are part of the hashed
bytes (group hash header, instance hash, CNF, coverage and checkpoint headers), so renaming them
would change every hash this document relies on. The native sources were not changed by the rename.

## 1. Definitions

A Pauli operator on n qubits, phases ignored, is a vector `v = (x | z)` in
GF(2)^2n (`x_q = 1` for X or Y on qubit q, `z_q = 1` for Z or Y). Two Paulis
commute iff the symplectic product `x·z' + z·x'` is 0 mod 2. The **Pauli
weight** `wt(v)` is the number of qubits with `x_q = 1` or `z_q = 1` (a Y
counts once).

A code is given by a list of generators `g_1..g_m` (dependent rows allowed).
They must pairwise commute (checked). `S` = their GF(2) span (the stabilizer
group modulo phases), `N(S)` = the Paulis commuting with every generator
(equivalently with all of S), `k = n - rank(S)`. For `k >= 1`

    d = min { wt(v) : v in N(S), v not in S }.

For `k = 0`, `N(S) = S` and no distance is defined (hyqec-verify reports
`no_logical`). Phases and sign consistency of the supplied generators are
outside this definition: any isotropic subspace has a consistent sign choice,
and d does not depend on it.

**Per-side (CSS) distances.** `d_X = min { wt(v) : v in N(S) minus S, z(v) = 0 }`
and `d_Z` likewise with `x(v) = 0`. For a CSS code these are the usual
X- and Z-distances (`x in ker H_Z minus rowspace H_X`), and `d = min(d_X, d_Z)`.
Why they coincide for CSS input: an X-only Pauli `(x|0)` is in `N(S)` iff
`H_Z x = 0`, and it is in `S = rowspace(H_X) ⊕ rowspace(H_Z)` (as `(x|0)`, `(0|z)`
blocks) iff `x in rowspace(H_X)`. For a non-CSS generating set, S has no such
split, and "the minimum weight of an X-only nontrivial logical" is not a standard
quantity. hyqec-verify therefore **rejects `--side X/Z` unless every given generator is
pure X or pure Z** (the API engines still accept a domain, for research use).

## 2. The trusted core (`src/hyqec_verify/core.py`)

Everything an engine outputs is re-checked here; every engine's input
(generators, logical basis) comes from here.

* **Linear algebra.** `GF2Basis` keeps a fully reduced echelon basis (pivot =
  highest bit; no row has a bit at another row's pivot). Reduction XORs exactly
  the rows whose pivots are set in the input, which is correct because no row
  changes another pivot position. The basis of a subspace is unique, which gives
  the canonical **group hash** (sha256 of the reduced basis of S).
* **Normalizer and logical basis.** `N(S)` is the common kernel of the
  functionals `v -> <v, g_i>`; its dimension must be `n + k` (checked). The
  logical basis `L` extends a basis of S by kernel vectors: exactly `2k` are
  independent modulo S (checked), they lie in `N(S)` (checked), and their
  symplectic Gram matrix has full rank (checked).
* **Nontriviality.** For `v in N(S)`: `v not in S` iff `<v, l> = 1` for some
  `l in L`. (=>: if v commutes with S and all of L it commutes with
  `span(S, L) = N(S)`, so v lies in `N(S)^perp = S`. <=: S commutes with N(S).)
  `is_logical` uses the direct definition (commutes with every generator, not in
  the span), not this equivalence.
* **Witness check.** A witness proves `d <= wt(v)` iff `is_logical(v)`.
* **Automorphisms.** A qubit permutation π is accepted iff it is a bijection of
  `0..n-1` and `π(g_i) in S` for every generator; then `π(S) ⊆ S` and equal
  dimension gives `π(S) = S`. Such π preserves `N(S)`, S and Pauli weight.

Tests: `tests/test_core.py` compares all of this with an independent brute-force
oracle (explicit group closure, scan of all 4^n Paulis) on random codes.

## 3. Symmetry: orbit roots

Let G be the group generated by verified automorphisms, `O_0, O_1, ...` its
qubit orbits in any fixed order, with representatives `rep_i in O_i`.

**Lemma (roots).** If a nontrivial logical of weight `<= W` exists, then one
exists that (a) contains `rep_i` in its support and (b) is the identity on every
qubit of `O_0..O_{i-1}`, for some i.
*Proof.* Take a minimum-weight nontrivial logical L*. Let i be the least orbit
index meeting `supp(L*)`, q a qubit of `supp(L*)` in `O_i`, π in G with
`π(q) = rep_i`. `π(L*)` is a nontrivial logical of the same weight, contains
`rep_i`, and avoids `O_0..O_{i-1}` because orbits are G-invariant. ∎

*The orbits must cover every qubit.* The proof needs `O_0..O_{m-1}` to be a partition of all
qubits into the G-orbits, numbered 0..m-1, with exactly one `rep_i in O_i` each. A qubit outside
every rooted orbit has no `c_i` that can select it, so the CNF silently excludes logicals the roots
do not cover and a valid DRAT proof certifies the wrong formula. `sat.encode` (and therefore
`hyqec-verify verify`, through `encode_from_params`) rejects any other orbit data; recorded orbit data
must also be the orbit partition of the re-verified group. This was a verify-path bug before release
(see CHANGELOG).

*Detection is presentation-dependent (completeness of the speed-up, not soundness).* Candidates come
from nauty on the Pauli-labelled Tanner graph of the *supplied* generators (or block-cyclic shifts), so
only automorphisms that permute the supplied generator set are found. A code given by recombined
generators can have symmetries that hyqec-verify does not see; the search is then slower, never wrong.
Presentation-independent detection would need a canonical sparse generating set; Clifford-labelled
automorphisms (per-qubit relabelling of X/Y/Z) are also not used. Both are on the roadmap.

With the trivial group every qubit is its own orbit and the roots just say
"qubit `rep_i` is the first supported qubit in the chosen order". Any order is
sound. Every permutation is stored in the certificate and re-verified.

## 4. SAT/DRAT lower bounds (`engines/sat.py`)

Encoding `stabcert-pauli-cnf/1` for a bound W (`0 <= W < n`):

1. variables `x_q`, `z_q`, `u_q` with clauses `(-x_q ∨ u_q)`, `(-z_q ∨ u_q)`,
   `(x_q ∨ z_q ∨ -u_q)`, i.e. `u_q ⇔ x_q ∨ z_q`;
2. domain X: units `-z_q`; domain Z: units `-x_q`;
3. for each generator g: `XOR { x_q : z_q(g) = 1 } ⊕ XOR { z_q : x_q(g) = 1 } = 0`;
4. for each `l in L`: a fresh `y_l` with `y_l = <v, l>` (as an XOR), and `OR_l y_l`;
5. `sum_q u_q <= W` (sequential counter, Sinz 2005);
6. optional orbit roots: fresh `c_i` with `c_i → u_{rep_i}`, `c_i → -u_q` for
   all q in `O_j`, `j < i`, and `OR_i c_i`.

XOR constraints are split into chains of at most `chunk` literals with Tseitin
variables, each piece expanded into its exact truth table (clauses forbidding
every assignment of the wrong parity), so every chain variable is a function of
the inputs.

**Theorem.** Without (6), the CNF is satisfiable iff there is a Pauli v in the
domain with `v in N(S)`, `v not in S`, `wt(v) <= W`; with (6) satisfiability is
unchanged.
*Proof.* Given such v, set x, z from v, u by (1), chain and selector variables
by their defining parities, the counter variables to the prefix sums: (3) holds
since v commutes with every generator, (4) since v is nontrivial (section 2),
(5) since `sum u = wt(v) <= W`. Conversely a model gives v satisfying (3), hence
`v in N(S)`; (4) gives some `<v, l> = 1`, so `v not in S`; (1) and (5) give
`wt(v) <= W`; (2) puts v in the domain. For (6): by the roots lemma (which only
uses existence), if any such v exists one exists that satisfies some `c_i`'s
implications; set that `c_i` true and the others false. ∎

So **UNSAT proves `d_domain >= W+1`.** hyqec-verify accepts UNSAT only if the
vendored `drat-trim` (optionally followed by LRAT checking with `lrat-check`)
reports `s VERIFIED` for a proof of the exact CNF bytes. Solver exit codes alone
never count. A SAT answer counts only through its model, which the core checks
as a witness.

**Cube bundles.** For cubes `C_1..C_r` (conjunctions of literals) hyqec-verify proves
`F ∧ C_j` UNSAT for every j and `F ∧ ¬C_1 ∧ ... ∧ ¬C_r` UNSAT (the coverage
formula), each with its own checked proof. If F had a model it would satisfy
some `C_j` (by the coverage proof), contradicting that leaf's proof. The cubes
are on the root selectors `c_i` (and optionally the root qubit's label); their
coverage is not assumed but proved.

**Re-checking.** `hyqec-verify verify` rebuilds the code from the generators listed
in the certificate, checks the group hash and k, re-verifies every permutation
and the orbit numbering, regenerates each CNF with the same encoder and
parameters, compares its sha256 with the record, and re-runs the checker on the
stored proof.

**Trusted for a SAT lower bound:** `core.py`, the encoder in `engines/sat.py`
(about 150 lines, `encode` and `Formula`), the vendored `drat-trim` (and
`lrat-check` if requested), the C compiler. Not trusted: the SAT solver, the
symmetry finder, nauty. The reduction above is a paper proof, not a
machine-checked one; `tests/test_sat.py` checks it exhaustively on small codes
(every Pauli, every W, every domain: CNF plus the Pauli's x/z units is
satisfiable exactly when the Pauli is a qualifying logical, decided by a small
independent DPLL).

## 5. Branch-and-bound lower bounds (`native/bnb/core.h`)

*Credit.* This search is not new. It follows the irreducible-cluster principle of
Dumer, Kovalev and Pryadko (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 63, 4675 (2017),
arXiv:1611.07164): a minimum-weight logical has no proper nonempty sub-support of
zero syndrome. It is essentially the general-stabilizer branch-and-bound of
Lee et al., arXiv:2609.30069, Appendix B.2: branch on the labels of an unsatisfied
(anticommuting) check, forbid the choices already tried, stop at zero syndrome,
and prune with a disjoint-available-set bound. The proof below is written for the
exact rules of `core.h`, including the restricted X/Z domains and the orbit roots.

A node is a partial assignment: every qubit is *decided* (I, X, Y or Z),
*restricted* to `{I, P}` for one Pauli P, or *free* (`{I,X,Y,Z}`). Its
*completions* are the Paulis whose label on each qubit lies in its domain.
`P_v` = the decided non-I labels, `w_v = wt(P_v)`, `A_v` = generators that
anticommute with `P_v`. A qubit q of generator g (label `g_q`) is a *candidate*
of g if its domain contains a non-I label different from `g_q` (exactly the
qubits that can still flip the commutation with g; two non-I single-qubit Paulis
anticommute iff they differ).

At each node: **dead** if some `g in A_v` has no candidate; **leaf** if `A_v` is
empty (then `P_v in N(S)`: report it if it anticommutes with some `l in L`,
never extend it); **bound**: discard if `w_v + h > cap` where h is the size of
a greedy packing of generators of `A_v` with pairwise disjoint candidate sets;
**branch** on the `g in A_v` with the fewest children: for its candidates
`q_1, q_2, ...` in g's fixed order, child i gives `q_i` a label anticommuting
with `g_{q_i}` and restricts every earlier candidate `q_j` to labels commuting
with `g_{q_j}` (free → `{I, g_{q_j}}`, restricted → I). Every child has weight
`w_v + 1`.

**Roots.** Root `(i, l)`: qubits of `O_0..O_{i-1}` decided I, `rep_i` decided l,
all others at their initial domain. Domain P: all qubits free, `l in {X,Y,Z}`.
Domain X (Z): all qubits restricted to `{I,X}` (`{I,Z}`), `l = X` (Z).

**Lemma 1 (irreducible support).** If L* is a minimum-weight element of
`N(S) minus S` within the domain, no nonempty proper subset T of `supp(L*)` has
`L*|_T in N(S)`. *Proof.* Otherwise `L*|_T` and `L*|_rest` are both in `N(S)`,
lighter than L*, in the domain (restrictions of a domain operator), and not both
in S (their product L* is not). ∎

**Theorem.** If the domain contains a nontrivial logical of weight `<= cap0`, the
search (min mode: on a find of weight f the cap becomes f−1) reports the minimum
weight; if nothing is reported, `d_domain >= cap0 + 1`.
*Proof.* Every report is a verified element of `N(S) minus S` (and is re-verified
by the core), so reported weights are `>= d`. Suppose the search ends without
reporting d. Then the cap stayed `>= d`. By the roots lemma some minimum-weight
L* is a completion of a root. Invariant for a node v with L* among its
completions, `L* = P_v ⊗ R`: for `g in A_v`, R anticommutes with g, so an odd
number of g's undecided qubits carry, in L*, a non-I label different from `g_q`;
those qubits are candidates. Hence (1) v is not dead; (2) if `A_v` is empty,
`P_v in N(S)` is a nonempty restriction of L*, so by Lemma 1 `P_v = L*`, which
is reported — contradiction; (3) each packed generator needs its own qubit of R
from disjoint candidate sets, so `w_v + h <= wt(L*) = d <= cap` and v is not
pruned (truncating the packing only lowers h); (4) for the branching generator
let `q_i` be the first candidate whose L*-label anticommutes with `g_{q_i}`; the
earlier candidates carry commuting labels, which is exactly child i's
restriction, so L* is a completion of child i. Children have weight `w_v + 1 <=
cap`, so the tree is finite and following L* ends in case (2). ∎

**Restricted domains (X / Z), explicitly.** Let `P0 = X` (resp. Z). Every qubit
starts *restricted* to `{I, P0}`; "completions" and "minimum" below are taken
over operators whose labels are all in `{I, P0}` (X-type, resp. Z-type).
Each step of the proof above is re-checked in this setting:

* *Roots.* A qubit permutation does not change labels, so `π(L*)` is again in
  the domain; the roots lemma (section 3) holds within the domain. Root
  `(i, P0)` decides `O_0..O_{i-1}` as I and `rep_i` as `P0`; the other labels are
  not roots because no domain operator has them. Every domain operator meeting
  `O_i` first is (after π) a completion of root `(i, P0)`.
* *Candidates.* A restricted qubit q of g with domain `{I, P0}` is a candidate iff
  `P0 != g_q`. If R (the undecided part of L*) anticommutes with g, an odd number
  of g's undecided qubits carry, in L*, a non-I label different from `g_q`; in the
  domain that label is `P0`, which lies in the qubit's current domain (L* is a
  completion), so `P0 != g_q` and the qubit is a candidate. Non-candidate qubits
  of g (`g_q = P0`) commute with g whatever their label in `{I, P0}`. Hence
  "dead" never discards a node with L* among its completions.
* *Children.* At a restricted candidate the only anticommuting label is `P0`
  (one child option, `options()` returns `a - LFREE`), and the rule for earlier
  candidates is "restricted → I". For L*, let `q_i` be the first candidate of the
  branching generator with an anticommuting L*-label (that label is `P0`); every
  earlier candidate `q_j` has an L*-label in `{I, P0}` commuting with `g_{q_j}`,
  and since `P0` anticommutes with `g_{q_j}` (q_j is a candidate) that label is I,
  exactly child i's restriction. So L* is a completion of exactly one child.
  Free qubits never occur in these searches, so no child ever leaves the domain.
* *Packing bound.* Unchanged: each packed generator needs a qubit of R from its
  own candidate set, with non-I label; the sets are disjoint, so `wt(R) >= h`.
* *Leaf / Lemma 1.* Restrictions of a domain operator are domain operators, so
  Lemma 1 holds with "minimum within the domain"; a leaf `P_v` is in `N(S)`, and
  the nontriviality test with the full logical basis is valid for every element
  of `N(S)` (section 2).

Therefore, with domain X (Z), "nothing reported at cap C" proves `d_X >= C+1`
(`d_Z >= C+1`), and a report of weight f proves `d_X = f` (`d_Z = f`).

**Task splitting, budgets, checkpoints.** The tree is cut at weight `split`;
each emitted node is rebuilt by replaying its decision path with the same code
(a replay mismatch is counted and must be 0). Abandoned over-budget tasks are
re-expanded so every node is explored exactly once; the total node count equals
an unsplit run (tested). A checkpoint is bound to the instance hash, domain,
cap, resolved split, delta, stop-first flag, source and binary hashes, and is refused
otherwise. Version-2 checkpoints chain each completion/find record using zlib CRC-32
from the header; the entire chain and task bookkeeping are checked before resume.
This detects accidental corruption, not deliberate edits with recomputed checksums.
Every chunk is flushed and synced, and close errors prevent a successful final result. A find in a checkpointed run is written durably (`fsync`) as a terminal
record and counts only as an upper bound; crash/resume behaviour, including torn
records at every byte offset, is tested in `tests/native/test_checkpoint_crash.py`
(invariants: `tests/native/CHECKPOINT_CRASH.md`).

**What a B&B certificate is.** Two kinds of B&B evidence exist.

* *Coverage certificate* (`--bnb-cert`, format in docs/BNB_CERT_SPEC.md): the explicit top of the
  search tree plus, for every frontier task, its decision path, node count and trace. It is not a
  succinct proof: an independent checker re-executes every task with its own implementation of the
  rules above. `hyqec-verify verify --bnb-checker CMD` first binds the file's header (group hash, domain,
  cap, split, instance hash) and the file's sha256 to the certificate's question, then counts the bound
  only if the checker exits 0 **and** prints a JSON report with `certificate_verified: true`. hyqec-verify
  does not parse the proof records itself: completeness of the file (every task present, the COMPLETE
  footer matching the independently accumulated counts and traces, no trailing bytes) is verified by
  the independent checker, and `certificate_verified: true` is its statement that it did so. A checker
  that exits 0 without that report, or reports a partial (sampled/ranged) replay, is not an acceptance. Coverage files produced outside hyqec-verify (e.g.
  by the native binary directly) can be wrapped with `hyqec-verify wrap-coverage`; they are checked the
  same way, and `--rerun-bnb` is refused for them (hyqec-verify did not write their instance).
* *Run record* (no `--bnb-cert`): instance hash, root permutations, engine source and binary hashes,
  node counts. `hyqec-verify verify` checks the permutations and orbit numbering but counts the lower bound
  only with `--rerun-bnb`, which re-executes the search and compares the outcome and node count (the
  tree is deterministic, identical on CPU and GPU, for runs without a find).

**Trusted for a B&B lower bound:** with an accepted coverage certificate, `core.py` (the code, the
witness) and the independent checker's implementation of the rules; without one, `core.py`, `core.h`
and its driver, the compiler (and CUDA toolchain for GPU runs). Neither is formally verified; both are
validated against the brute-force and SAT engines and the independent reference corpus.

**Adding pruning.** Any new pruning rule or option (an extra bound, a cache, a symmetry reduction)
may be enabled for certificates or default runs only after it passes the *differential cut test*:
every subtree the new rule cuts is re-explored by the search without that rule, under the same cap,
and must contain no nontrivial logical of weight <= cap. Agreement with reference distances is not
sufficient (the test: `tests/native/test_pruning.py::test_differential_every_cut_on_2000_random_codes`, documented in
`tests/native/PRUNING_AUDIT.md`, which
re-runs every cut on 2000+ random codes, with an injected-unsafe-cut self-test). Example: an unsound lifted-cache prune reported d >= 4 on an [[18,1,3]] code
(`tests/inputs/lifted_cache_counterexample.json`, now a package acceptance test) while agreeing with
many reference distances. Version-1 coverage certificates admit only the rules of this section.

The differential audit must cover **every buffer-size regime of every build variant** in which the rule
is compiled, not only small codes. A rule with fixed-size tables, touched lists or caches must:
- give, for each of them, the index bound and the cleanup argument (state that must be clean between calls
  is restored on every return path) at the variant's NMAX/MMAX/WMAX/CMAX;
- have every capacity limit that can occur in a variant crossed at full size in that variant, with the
  hits counted and asserted (a search-level stratum where a search at feasible caps reaches the limit, a
  node-level stratum with planted completions otherwise);
- also pass the small-code audit with every table shrunk, so that each overflow path runs under the
  enumeration oracle;
- have a runtime check of the between-call invariants after every rule call.
Small random codes alone can miss defects that need large n (review finding 01: an S1 cleanup list that only
overflowed above n = 2048). For S0/S1/S2 this is `tests/native/port/SCRATCH_AUDIT.md` with the tests listed
there.

### 5.1 Opt-in parity rules S0, S1, S2 (`--bound greedy+s0 | greedy+s0s1 | greedy+s0s1s2`)

*Status.* Opt-in, **CPU only** (`--gpu` is refused), compiled only into a separate CPU *rules build*
(`-DSTABCERT_RULES`, native variant suffix `-rules`, selected automatically by `engines/bnb.py` for these
policies; its `--version` limits carry `"RULES":"s0s1s2/1"`). Every default build (CPU 1024/4096/w64/w128
and CUDA) preprocesses to exactly the previous search code, so the default trees, node counts and
coverage payloads are unchanged by construction. **Independently checked only through coverage
certificate v2**: coverage certificate v1 does not admit these rules (a v1 attachment on such a record
fails verification). With `--bnb-cert` they write a version-2 certificate (docs/BNB_CERT_SPEC_v2.md,
revision 2.0-rc1), and a counted lower bound comes either from `verify --rerun-bnb` (same-prover re-execution
with exactly the recorded policy) or from `verify --bnb-checker` accepting that v2 certificate with the
companion checker hyqec-check >= 2.0.0 (`hyqec-check-v2`), which re-executes every subtree with the declared
rule set and its own implementation of S0/S1/S2. `verify` derives each B&B item's evidence level from the
method, `params.bound` and the checker verdict (no extra field), e.g. "lower bound by bnb rerun, policy
greedy+s0s1, same-prover re-execution, not independently checked" or "lower bound by bnb coverage
certificate v2 (rules s0s1s2, spec 2.0-rc1), policy greedy+s0s1s2, independently checked by hyqec-check-v2
(reported version 2.0.0)"; the label "independently checked" comes only from a coverage certificate accepted by the
independent checker. Binding (section 5.2).
Unknown policy names fail. They are not the default; the default `greedy` tree is unchanged.
Origin: S0 and S1 come from one author of the companion exact-distance study, S2 from another;
independent reviews: S0 (with a prior-art review), the fixed S1 (after a reviewer found the
stale-cache defect described below), and S2. The code is a port of the reviewed sources
(`native/bnb/s012.inc`, `native/bnb/s2.inc`, regenerated byte for byte from them by
`tests/native/port/gen_s012.py`; deviations listed at the end of this section).

*What each rule claims.* All three rules are **refutations of completions**: they discard a node v only
when v has **no zero-syndrome completion `P_v ⊗ R` with `wt(P_v ⊗ R) <= cap`** (domain-respecting, R on
undecided qubits). This is stronger than "no nontrivial logical in the subtree" and needs no
nontriviality argument. It is exactly what case (3) of the Theorem uses: the minimum-weight logical L*
(weight `d <= cap`) is a zero-syndrome completion of every node on its path, so no rule discards such a
node, and the Theorem's proof goes through with "not pruned" covering the new rules too. In the cut
audit every cut by these rules is checked against complete enumeration and against a rule-free
re-search (section 5, *Adding pruning*).

Notation for a node v (after the weight cut, so `r = cap − w_v >= 1`): `A_v` the anticommuting
generators; `C_g` the candidate qubits of g; a *packing* `g_1..g_h` = generators of `A_v` with pairwise
disjoint candidate sets; for packed `g_i`, `D_i` = actions `(q, l)` with `q in C_i`, l in q's domain,
`l != I`, `l` anticommuting with `g_i|q`. `σ(a)` = the syndrome of a single action `a`; `t_g = [g in A_v]`.

**Packing (fused with the greedy bound).** The rules compute the greedy packing of section 5 in the same
order (so `h > r` prunes exactly as `bound()` does), then, if it is smaller than r, a threshold search
`mis_pack` for a larger packing on the "share a candidate qubit" graph of `A_v` (|A_v| <= 64:
min-degree greedy, a greedy clique cover by qubit cliques to skip hopeless searches, then a bitmask
branch and bound towards the target). Every packing it returns is a valid disjoint packing, so the
section-5 bound remains valid for it (`h > r` prunes). It is a threshold routine, **not** a maximum
independent set: it may stop below the maximum (known limit 2).

**S0 (slack 0, `h = r`).** *Lemma.* If v has a zero-syndrome completion `L = P_v ⊗ R` with
`wt(R) <= r` then (i) `|supp R ∩ C_i| = 1` for every i, `supp R ⊆ ∪ C_i`, and R's action `x_i` in `C_i`
lies in `D_i`; (ii) for every generator g: `#{i : x_i flips g} ≡ t_g (mod 2)`.
*Proof.* `g_i in A_v` and L commutes with `g_i`, so R anticommutes with `g_i`: an odd (>= 1) number of
R's qubits in `g_i`'s support carry a label anticommuting with `g_i`; each such qubit is a candidate of
`g_i`. The `C_i` are disjoint, so `wt(R) >= Σ |R ∩ C_i| >= r >= wt(R)`; equality forces one qubit per
`C_i`, nothing outside, and that qubit anticommutes with `g_i`. (ii): R anticommutes with g iff `P_v`
does. ∎
So the `x_i` are the variables of a parity CSP with domains `D_i` and one parity constraint per
generator. The rule prunes when (a) *unit propagation* refutes it: a value `x_i = (q, l)` is removed if
some generator `k != g_i` at q gets the wrong parity from it and no other variable has any value (in
its **original** domain at v) that flips k, or if some `k` with `t_k = 1` can be flipped only by
variable i and this value does not flip it; an odd unpacked generator that no variable can flip refutes
directly; or (b) *generalised arc consistency* (GAC) over all parity constraints wipes out a domain.
Both only remove values that no solution of (i)–(ii) uses, so an empty domain means no completion of
weight <= cap exists. At a table limit they drop constraints (fewer removals) or give up (no
prune); either only weakens the rule.

**S1 (slack 1, `h = r − 1`).** *Lemma.* A completion R with `wt(R) <= h + 1` is either an S0 solution
for the packing (weight h), or `R = {x_1..x_h} ∪ {E}` with `x_i in D_i`, one further action E on an
undecided qubit not used by any `x_i`, **E flips no packed generator**, and for every generator g:
`#{i : x_i flips g} + [E flips g] ≡ t_g`.
*Proof.* As in S0 each `C_i` holds an odd number >= 1 of R's qubits anticommuting with `g_i`, so at most
one qubit E remains, outside `∪ C_i` (not a candidate of any packed g, so it cannot flip one) or a
second qubit of some `C_j` (then exactly one of the two anticommutes with `g_j`, call it `x_j`, and E
does not flip `g_j`). Parity as in S0. ∎
*Killers.* Killers are computed in **one pass against the original domains at v**, with no chaining:
`Fl(k)` = the variables having some value in their original `D_j` that flips k. A value `c = (q, l)` of
variable i is *killed* by k if (a) k is at q, `k != g_i`, `[c flips k] != t_k` and `Fl(k) ⊆ {i}`, or
(b) `t_k = 1`, `Fl(k) = {i}` and c does not flip k. `Z` = unpacked k with `t_k = 1` and `Fl(k) = ∅`.
*Lemma (killer).* In a case-(b) completion with `x_i = c`, E flips every killer of c, and E flips every
`k in Z`. *Proof.* k's parity is `Σ_j [x_j flips k] + [E flips k] = t_k`; each `x_j` lies in the
original `D_j`, so only `j in Fl(k) ⊆ {i}` can contribute. In (a) the sum without E is
`[c flips k] != t_k`; in (b) it is 0 while `t_k = 1`; for `k in Z` it is 0. ∎ No other value's removal
enters the argument, so no chain can revive c.
*Test.* (1) Unit analysis of the S0 case; if it does not refute, do not prune. (2) Candidate E must:
flip all of Z; for every wiped variable, flip all killers of **some** value of it; lie (qubit
intersection filter) in the support of a first-recorded killer of a value of every wiped variable and of
every `z in Z`; flip no packed generator; every even generator it makes odd must have some variable able
to flip it. Each is a necessary condition, so discarding E that fails one is sound. (3) For every
surviving E, unit analysis with targets `t xor σ(E)` and E's qubit removed from every domain (a
refutation of case (b) for that E, by the same lemma). Prune iff (1) and all (3) refute.
*Defect found in review and fixed.* A reviewer found that after the per-E re-run (computed with E's qubit
removed from ownership) the owners cache still held values with that qubit excluded, so the candidate
pre-filter read stale `Fl(k) = ∅` and could reject a valid E: a false prune, lifted to a false
"d >= 4" on an [[18,1,3]] code. Fix: invalidate the cache (`++curstamp`) after restoring ownership. The
lifted counterexample (`tests/native/fixtures/pruning/lifted-cache-counterexample.inst`) and the
offending node (`tests/native/rule_cases.cpp`, `s1-stale-cache-node`) are permanent regressions.

**S2 (slack 2, `h = r − 2`).** *Lemma (equivalence).* A zero-syndrome completion R with
`wt(R) <= r` exists iff there are an extra set E of zero, one or two actions (on distinct undecided
qubits, labels in their domains) and representatives `x_i in D_i` with: no representative on a qubit of
E; `XOR_{a in E} π(a) = 0` (π = syndrome on the packed rows); and
`XOR_i σ(x_i) XOR XOR_{a in E} σ(a) = σ(P_v)` on all generators.
*Proof.* Choose for each packed row one of R's (odd, >= 1) actions anticommuting with it as `x_i`;
disjointness makes them distinct, and each has `π = e_i`; the rest E has size `wt(R) − h <= 2`, and
`π(E) = 0` because `π(R)` and `π({x_i})` are both all-ones. Conversely such a tuple uses at most
`h + |E| <= r` distinct qubits and has syndrome `σ(P_v)`, so it completes `P_v` to zero syndrome. ∎
*Affine-quotient filter (what is implemented).* Fix a base value `b_i` in each **original** `D_i` and
let `W` = span of `σ(x) XOR σ(b_i)` over all values of all `D_i`. Every representative sum lies in
`B + W` with `B = XOR_i σ(b_i)`, so a necessary condition on E is `σ(E) + σ(P_v) + B in W`. The code
tests this condition **exactly**: it accepts E = ∅ iff `σ(P_v) + B in W`, a single action a iff
`σ(a) + σ(P_v) + B in W`, and a pair on distinct qubits iff `σ(a) + σ(b) + σ(P_v) + B in W`. A linear map H
with kernel ⊇ W (a 64-bit fingerprint of the quotient by W) is only a filter: E with a fingerprint mismatch
cannot satisfy the condition, and every fingerprint match is confirmed by exact reduction modulo an echelon
basis of W. The decision is therefore a pure function of the node state, independent of the keys of H. The
conditions "no representative on E's qubits" and `π(E) = 0` are dropped (a relaxation), so a node is pruned
only when **no** E of size 0, 1 or 2 satisfies the necessary condition:
then no completion of weight <= cap exists. No propagated domains or killer chains are used. The rule is
dispatched only at `h = r − 2`; at `h = r − 1` S1 runs and at `h = r` S0 runs (each rule is tried only
at its own slack). It gives up (no prune) when `n > 1365` (its hash table holds `3n` actions).

**Known limits (performance, not soundness).** Each limit only loses prunes:
1. *GAC is incomplete.* An inconsistent XOR cycle among packed variables (a reviewer's 7-qubit example,
   `rule_cases.cpp: s0-gac-incomplete`; the node has no completion, GAC sees >= 2 "mixed" variables per
   constraint and cannot refute) is not pruned. "S0 = oracle-1" (pruning every futile tight node) was
   observed on the benchmark instances only; it is not a theorem.
2. *The packing search is a threshold, not a maximum.* `mis_pack` can stop at 3 when the maximum is 4
   (`rule_cases.cpp: s0-mis-threshold`); the slack seen by the rules can therefore be larger than the
   true slack, and which rule applies depends on the packing found.
3. *Killers and filters use the original domains at the node* (S1, S2): no propagation chains; the
   per-E S1 analysis is unit propagation only; S2 is a linear relaxation.
4. *Table limits.* Every capacity limit (choice / occurrence tables, |A_v| > 64 for `mis_pack`, more
   than 31 packed variables in S1's bitmask, the S2 size guard) returns "no prune". This needs the scratch
   state that persists between calls to be clean again after every call, whatever limit was hit. The ported
   S1 filter originally broke that at NMAX > 2048 (review finding 01: a 2048-entry cleanup list, then a false
   S1 prune on a later node). It was fixed by recording every dirtied qubit. Every fixed-size array, with its
   bound and cleanup argument for all build variants, is in `tests/native/port/SCRATCH_AUDIT.md`. The cut
   audit checks the cleanup after every rule pass, also with all tables shrunk so the overflow paths run on
   small codes.
5. The research driver's exact CSP check had a 1e5-node budget; it is not part of this code.

**Port deviations** (from the reviewed `frozen/v2-s1fix-2f08f4/core.h`, sha256 2f08f408…, reviewed with
`-DCSP0 -DMISPACK -DUNITFIRST -DFUSED -DMINDEG -DS1 -DS1MINR=2`, and the reviewed `frozen/v1-c54147fb`
`fast.inc`, sha256 126594f8…, `-DS2`; each change is marked `[port]` in the source):
compile-time flags became the runtime level `Code::prune` (1 = S0, 2 = +S1, 3 = +S2) with the same order
of tests; `csp_prune` returns which rule cut (for the cut-audit hook and the trace word); `na[]` widened
from 8 to 16 bits (wide variants allow `2 * WMAX` values per variable); the `mis_pack` scratch arrays
moved from the stack into the search state; statistics, timing and unused experimental code (a second
GAC variant, an unused bound, flipper lists, the S2 audit counters) were removed; `Dfs::init` clears
the rule scratch when a rule policy is active (the research driver relied on zero-initialised

(`S2_HT`, default 8192 as reviewed) are overridable for the capacity-boundary test build. Integration (`core.h`, all under `#ifdef STABCERT_RULES`): `enter_rules()` / `run_rules()` are
`enter()` / `run()` with the fused rule pass `csp_prune` in place of `bound()`, exactly as the reviewed

**Checkpoints.** For these policies the checkpoint header additionally binds ` bound=<policy>`; a resume

### 5.2 Consuming a coverage certificate version 2

What `verify --bnb-checker` requires before a version-2 attachment (`SBCOV2`, header `format`
`stabcert-bnb-coverage/2`) counts as `cap + 1` (`certificate.py`, `_check_coverage`):

1. the file's sha256 equals the recorded one;
2. the header binds the question: `group_sha256` is the re-verified code's group hash, `domain`, `cap`,
   `instance_sha256` and `split` equal the record, the identifiers are exactly `irreducible-pauli-dfs/2`,
   `poly64-state-rules/2`, `rules_version` 1, and `rules` is the rule set the recorded policy writes
   (`greedy` -> `greedy`, `greedy+s0` -> `s0`, `greedy+s0s1` -> `s0s1`, `greedy+s0s1s2` -> `s0s1s2`;
   `none` and unknown policies fail). The policy is never taken from the header alone;
3. the configured checker exits 0 and reports `certificate_verified: true` (the JSON boolean). With
   `--bnb-checker auto`, an executable path or a checker source directory, hyqec-verify builds the command
   itself and passes `--expected-group/-instance/-domain/-cap` and `--threads` (`auto` selects
   `hyqec-check-v2` from hyqec-check >= 2.0.0); a user command template is run as given;
4. its verdict is about this question: `status` `accepted`, `selection` `all`, and `group_sha256`,
   `instance_sha256`, `domain`, `cap`, `rules`, `rules_version` and `spec_revision` (`2.0-rc1`) are present
   and equal to the certificate's, with exact JSON types (strings, and integers that are not booleans);
5. it reports `checker_version` as a version string >= 2.0.0. The evidence label repeats the configured
   checker's name and this reported version; both are metadata from the configured program, not
   authentication. Choosing the checker program is part of the trusted configuration (as for version 1).

For version 1 (checked with the frozen greedy profile, spec revision 1.1) steps 1-3 apply with the
version-1 identifiers, and of the verdict exactly six fields are checked when the checker reports them:
`group_sha256`, `instance_sha256`, `domain`, `cap`, `status` (`accepted`) and `selection` (`all`). Older
checkers that omit them are still accepted; `rules`, `rules_version` and `spec_revision` are not bound or
validated (`rules` and `spec_revision` are only copied into the verify report).

Anything else (no checker, a version-1 checker on a version-2 file, a missing field, a partial replay, a
tampered file) leaves the bound withheld. The trust is that of version 1 (section 5): the specification's coverage theorem for
the declared rule set, and the checker's independent implementation of it, including S0/S1/S2.

## 6. Brute force (`engines/brute.py`)

Enumerates every support of size 1, 2, ... in lexicographic order with every
label choice in the domain and tests the XOR of precomputed single-qubit
syndromes (generators and logical basis). The first weight with a hit is the
minimum; completing weight W without a hit proves `d_domain >= W+1`. Each hit is
re-checked with `is_logical`. Used for small codes and as a cross-check.

## 7. Upper bounds

RIS (Python, native CPU, CUDA) and SAT models only propose operators. An upper
bound is the weight of an operator that the core has verified; nothing about
the search procedure needs to be trusted.

## 8. Combining evidence

`distance` keeps the largest proven lower bound and the lightest verified
witness. A lower bound above a verified witness weight, or two engines that
disagree, yields status `inconsistent` (exit code 3); nothing is overridden.
`exact` means a verified witness of weight w and a proof that nothing of weight
`<= w-1` exists. Cross-checking (`--cross-check sat` after `--engines bnb`, or
vice versa) re-proves the final bound with an algorithmically independent engine;
the certificate then carries both records.

## 9. Not covered

* Formal (machine-checked) proofs of the reductions and of `core.h`.
* Distances of subsystem codes, dressed/bare distances, or circuit-level distances.
* Signed generators: phase keys are rejected, not interpreted.
